## 1. KEY LEGISLATION

### 1.1 Fair Work Act 2009 (and Closing Loopholes Amendments)

**What it is:** Australia's primary federal employment legislation governing pay, conditions, and workplace rights.

**Recent amendments (Closing Loopholes Acts):**
- **Fair Work Legislation Amendment (Closing Loopholes) Act 2023** — Royal Assent 14 December 2023
- **Fair Work Legislation Amendment (Closing Loopholes No. 2) Act 2024** — Royal Assent 26 February 2024

**Key changes relevant to reward/rem:**
- **Criminal wage theft offence (1 January 2025):** Intentional underpayment is now a criminal offence carrying up to **10 years imprisonment**. Fines: up to **$7.825M per offence for individuals**, **$39.125M for corporations**. Each employee underpaid, and each pay period, can be a separate offence.
- **Right to Disconnect (26 August 2024 for large employers; 26 August 2025 for small business):** Employees can refuse contact outside working hours. Penalties up to $18,780 (individuals) / $93,900 (corporations) for breach of orders.
- **Casual conversion rights (26 February 2025):** Casual employees can notify employer of intent to convert to permanent.
- **NES Review (November 2025):** First comprehensive review of the Fair Work Act since 2009 — further changes likely.

### 1.2 Workplace Gender Equality Act 2012 (WGEA)

**What it is:** Requires employers with 100+ employees to report annually to the Workplace Gender Equality Agency on gender composition, pay equity, and related metrics.

**Major 2025-2026 changes:**

- **Workplace Gender Equality Amendment (Setting Gender Equality Targets) Act 2025** — passed March 2025
- **Employers with 500+ employees** must select and work towards **3 gender equality targets** over a 3-year cycle, choosing from 19 legislated targets (at least 1 must be numeric)
- **Target submission deadlines:**
  - Private sector: **1 April – 31 May 2026**
  - Public sector: **1 September – 31 October 2026**
- Targets can relate to: gender pay gaps, flexible work, promotion rates, representation in management, sexual harassment safeguards
- **All employers with 100+ employees** must lodge annual WGEA reports containing gender, pay, and occupation data for each employee
- **Employer gender pay gaps are now published** — WGEA publishes organisation-level gender pay gap data publicly

**Why this matters for selling:**
Companies need systems that can produce gender pay gap analytics, track progress against numeric targets, and generate WGEA-compliant reporting. Spreadsheets cannot do this reliably at scale.

### 1.3 APRA Prudential Standard CPS 511 — Remuneration

**What it is:** Applies to all APRA-regulated entities (banks, insurers, super funds). Sets requirements for remuneration governance, variable pay design, and risk alignment.

**Timeline:**
- Large SFIs: compliant from **1 January 2023**
- Non-SFIs: compliant from **1 January 2024**
- **First disclosures due:** 30 June 2025 (Dec year-end entities) / 31 December 2025 (June year-end entities)

**Requirements:**
- Remuneration arrangements must appropriately incentivise prudent risk management
- Variable remuneration must include deferral and malus/clawback provisions (for SFIs)
- Disclosure of remuneration design, governance, and variable pay details
- Proposed ASX Corporate Governance Principles (5th ed) Recommendation 8.3 would add malus/clawback disclosure requirements

## 2. SUPERANNUATION COMPLIANCE

### 2.1 Payday Super (1 July 2026) — MAJOR CHANGE

**Legislation:** Passed 6 November 2025. Effective **1 July 2026**.

**What changes:**
- Super must be paid **at the same time as wages** (within 7 days of payday), replacing the current quarterly payment cycle
- Applies to weekly, fortnightly, and monthly pay cycles
- Employers must report QE (Qualifying Earnings) components used to calculate SG via real-time STP reporting
- Non-compliance triggers the **Superannuation Guarantee Charge (SGC)** — with updated interest and penalty framework

**Current SG rate:** **12%** of ordinary time earnings (from 1 July 2025)

**Compliance statistics:** ~9.3% of employers (~87,200) fail to pay SG without ATO intervention

**ATO compliance approach:** Draft practical compliance guidelines (PCG 2025/D5) outline a risk-based approach for the first 12 months, with priority enforcement on higher-risk employers.

**Why this matters for selling:**
Moving from quarterly to per-pay-period super calculation and payment is a massive operational change. Companies using spreadsheets or legacy systems for reward calculations will struggle to comply. Real-time STP reporting of QE components requires integrated systems.

## 3. ENFORCEMENT ACTIONS & HIGH-PROFILE CASES

### 3.1 Woolworths & Coles — ~$1 Billion Underpayment

- Federal Court (September 2025) found both retailers failed to keep accurate employment records, systematically underpaying **27,000+ workers**
- **Woolworths:** Already repaid ~$486M, expects to pay a further ~$530M
- **Coles:** Repaid $31M, estimates further $150–250M liability
- **Root cause:** Salaried staff on annualised salary arrangements — employers did not track or pay per-period Award entitlements (overtime, penalties). Relied on contractual set-off clauses, which the Court ruled insufficient
- Further directions hearing scheduled early 2026

### 3.2 Other Notable Cases

- **Qantas:** Discovered underpayment of 55 workers during review
- **Commonwealth Bank:** $4.8M owed to 8,000 employees
- **$4M penalty case (2025):** Federal Court imposed $4M in penalties for "calculated" wage theft — behaviour described as "deceitful and unscrupulous"

### 3.3 Enforcement Trends

- FWO reported a **34% increase in wage theft complaints** in the first 6 months of 2025 vs. same period 2024
- Priority enforcement sectors: fast food, retail, hospitality, horticulture, cleaning
- FWO released **Payroll Remediation Program Guide** (May 2025) — framework for self-reporting underpayments
- Regulators now assess whether organisations had **appropriate systems and controls**, not just whether underpayments occurred

## 4. PAYROLL & AWARD COMPLEXITY

### 4.1 Australia = 3rd Most Complex Payroll Environment Globally

- Australia rose from **11th to 3rd** in the 2025 Global Payroll Complexity Index — a **21% increase** in complexity score
- 122 modern awards, each with distinct pay rates, allowances, overtime rules, penalty rates
- Average spend on payroll compliance audits: **$272,000** (2025); employers with 5,000+ employees average **$434,439**
- **77% of employers** now deploy AI to detect compliance issues
- Only **43% of businesses** employ dedicated payroll professionals
- **36% cite lack of automation** as a leading cause of errors

### 4.2 Modern Award Interpretation Challenges

- Most underpayments are **unintentional** — driven by award complexity and system limitations
- Hybrid/remote work increases classification complexity
- Annualised salary arrangements require per-period reconciliation (the Woolworths/Coles lesson)
- Enterprise agreements add another layer of complexity on top of awards

## 6. SELLING IMPLICATIONS — URGENCY DRIVERS FOR HEAD OF REWARD / DIRECTOR OF REMUNERATION

### The Case Against Spreadsheets

1. **Criminal liability is personal.** Wage theft is now a criminal offence. "We used a spreadsheet and made an error" is not a defence. Regulators assess whether you had **appropriate systems and controls** — a spreadsheet is not a system.

2. **Woolworths/Coles precedent.** The $1B+ remediation bill resulted from failure to track per-period Award entitlements for salaried staff. Any company using annualised salaries without systematic reconciliation faces the same risk.

3. **WGEA targets require analytics.** By May 2026, companies with 500+ employees must select numeric gender equality targets and track progress. This requires pay gap analysis capability that spreadsheets cannot deliver reliably.

4. **Payday Super (July 2026).** Moving super from quarterly to per-pay-period requires integrated calculation and real-time STP reporting. Manual processes will break.

5. **Audit costs are rising.** Average compliance audit spend is $272K (up to $434K for large employers). Automated systems reduce audit burden and demonstrate "appropriate controls."

6. **Complexity is increasing, not decreasing.** Australia is now the 3rd most complex payroll environment globally. With 122 modern awards, hybrid work, and cascading legislative changes, manual reward management is an accelerating risk.

### Key Pain Questions for Discovery

- "How are you planning to meet the WGEA gender equality target requirements by May 2026? What system produces your pay gap analytics?"
- "With Payday Super starting in July 2026, how are you handling the shift from quarterly to per-period super calculation?"
- "After the Woolworths/Coles ruling, how confident are you that your annualised salary arrangements are being reconciled per pay period?"
- "If the Fair Work Ombudsman asked you to demonstrate your systems and controls for wage compliance today, what would you show them?"
- "What did your last payroll compliance audit cost, and what was the main finding?"

### Reframe / Commercial Insight (Challenger)

> "Most Heads of Reward think of compliance as a payroll problem. But since January 2025, it's a criminal liability problem — and the regulators have shifted from asking 'did you underpay?' to 'did you have appropriate systems and controls to prevent it?' A spreadsheet is a tool, not a control. The companies that will avoid the next Woolworths situation are the ones that can demonstrate systematic governance of their reward processes — not just accurate pay runs."
